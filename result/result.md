## 1. Minimum Distance Analysis of H₂O₂ Accessibility

### Table 1. Overall intermolecular separation between H₂O₂ and the tested compoundsfrom MD simulations.

| Compound | Min distance to H₂O₂ |
|---|---|
| Rosavin | 1.83 nm |
| L-arabinose | 2.11 nm |
| Rosin | 2.75 nm |

Table 1 summarizes the overall intermolecular separation between H₂O₂ and the tested compounds obtained from MD simulations using center-of-mass (COM) distance analysis. These values represent the average overall distance between the molecules rather than direct atom-to-atom contact distances. Rosavin showed the shortest COM distance to H₂O₂ (1.83 nm), indicating relatively higher accessibility to H₂O₂ compared with L-arabinose and rosin. In contrast, rosin exhibited the largest separation distance (2.75 nm), suggesting the weakest accessibility. Although none of the compounds showed strong stable direct binding interactions with H₂O₂ at the global molecular level, transient local contacts between specific atoms could still occur during the simulation, as observed in the minimum atom-to-atom distance analysis. Therefore, the antioxidant activity observed in the experiments may not be explained solely by simple stable binding interactions, but also by dynamic transient molecular contacts and accessibility to H₂O₂.

## 2. Radial Distribution Function (RDF)

Rosin showed the strongest local accumulation around H₂O₂, while rosavin also exhibited a relatively high RDF peak. In contrast, L-(+)-arabinose showed the weakest accumulation.

However, despite its high RDF peak, rosin demonstrated relatively low antioxidant activity experimentally.

These findings suggest that antioxidant activity is not determined solely by molecular proximity to H₂O₂, but is also influenced by hydration structure and solvent-mediated stabilization effects.

## 3. Hydrogen Bond Analysis (solute–water)

Hydrogen-bond analysis between the solutes and surrounding water molecules revealed that rosavin formed the largest hydration network among the tested compounds.

This suggests that the superior antioxidant activity of rosavin may be associated with enhanced solvent-mediated stabilization rather than direct binding to H₂O₂.

One-way ANOVA revealed highly significant differences in solute–water hydrogen-bond formation among the tested compounds:

- F = 974.7
- p < 0.001
